Common questions

Which devices does LateFrame work on?

iPhone and iPad with iOS 17 or later. One purchase covers both.

How does the delayed replay work?

Start Live replay and put the device where it can see you. The replay plays 10 seconds behind live, so you can finish the skill and walk back to watch it. The camera keeps the last 2 minutes by default (30 seconds to 5 minutes in Settings). Swipe right to go back in time and left to go forward. Tap the delay at the top to jump to live, and tap it again to go back to where you were. Pause and swipe slowly to go through a skill frame by frame.

How do I use two devices, one filming and one watching?

On the device that films, tap Use as camera. It shows a 4-digit code. On the other device, tap Watch camera, choose the camera and enter the code. Both devices need Wi-Fi turned on, but they don't need to be on a network: they connect directly to each other, so the gym's Wi-Fi isn't needed. Allow Local Network when asked. Once the picture is framed, tap Done to let the filming device sleep and save battery; it keeps filming.

The two devices don't find each other or lose the connection

Check that Wi-Fi is on on both devices, that both have Local Network allowed in Settings › Apps › LateFrame, and that the code matches the one on the filming device. Keep the devices within sight of each other; walls and long distances weaken the connection. If the connection drops, the watching device reconnects by itself.

Where are my videos saved?

When you save, you choose Photos, LateFrame's library, or both. The library is a folder on your device that you can also open in the Files app, under On My iPhone › LateFrame or On My iPad › LateFrame. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

The camera or microphone isn't working

Check that LateFrame is allowed to use them in Settings › Apps › LateFrame. Without the microphone, clips are saved without sound.

Which video quality should I choose?

1080p at 30 fps is the default and the best choice for long sessions. 60 fps gives twice as many frames for slow motion and uses more battery. 4K (up to 60 fps) and 120 fps are for short sessions: they drain the battery quickly, warm the device and use a lot of memory, so at 4K the longest delay can be shorter on some devices. When two devices are used, the quality goes up to 1080p at 60 fps. If a camera can't record the setting you chose, for example 4K on some front cameras, LateFrame uses the closest one it can and says so in Settings.

Using LateFrame on an iPad

On an iPad, the controls sit in the corners and the speeds are buttons. Put the iPad on a stand, and after a few seconds without a touch the controls fade and the delay, speed and hold time show large enough to read from a few metres. Touch the screen to bring the controls back. With an Apple Pencil you can draw on the picture while your finger moves through the replay.

My device gets warm during long sessions

Filming for a long time warms any phone or tablet. If the device gets hot, LateFrame lowers the frame rate to 30 fps until it has cooled down. Keeping it out of direct sunlight and off chargers helps.

How do I get a refund?

Purchases are handled by Apple. Request a refund at reportaproblem.apple.com.
